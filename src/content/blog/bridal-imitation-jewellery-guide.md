Most Indian weddings are not one event. There is the haldi, the mehendi, the sangeet, the wedding itself and often a reception. Each one has a different mood, a different outfit and ideally a different jewellery look. Imitation jewellery is what makes that possible without the cost of real gold for every function.

Here is how to plan it.

## Start with the outfits

Jewellery should come after the outfits, not before. Once your outfits are final, note down for each one:

- The **colour** and the **metal tone** that suits it (gold, antique gold or silver tone)
- The **neckline**, which decides the necklace shape
- Whether your **hair** will be open, in a bun or braided, which decides earrings and tikka

## Function by function

### Haldi

Keep it light and bright. Floral jewellery, small [earrings](/imitation-jewellery/earrings/) or simple bangles work well. Avoid your best pieces, because turmeric stains.

### Mehendi

Your hands will be busy for hours, so skip heavy bangles and rings. Go for statement earrings, a light necklace and perhaps a small [maang tikka](/imitation-jewellery/maang-tikka/). [Oxidised pieces](/collections/oxidised-jewellery/) and colourful stones suit the mood.

### Sangeet

This is the dance night. Choose pieces that stay put: secure earrings, a choker instead of a long swinging necklace, and a bracelet in place of a full bangle stack. [American diamond](/collections/american-diamond-jewellery/) looks great under stage lights.

### Wedding ceremony

This is the full bridal look:

1. A heavy [necklace set](/imitation-jewellery/necklace-sets/), often a choker layered with a long haar
2. Matching jhumkas or chandbalis
3. A bridal maang tikka, sometimes with a matha patti
4. A [bangle stack](/imitation-jewellery/bangles-bracelets/) with kadas at the ends
5. Rings, a nath and haath phool if they are part of your tradition

[Kundan](/collections/kundan-jewellery/) is the classic choice here, with [temple](/collections/temple-jewellery/) and [antique](/collections/antique-jewellery/) sets popular in South Indian weddings.

### Reception

Evening light and a western or Indo-western outfit usually mean American diamond. One statement necklace with drop earrings is often enough.

## A simple planning table

| Function | Necklace | Earrings | Extras |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Haldi | Light or none | Small, bright | Floral pieces |
| Mehendi | Light | Statement | Tikka, no heavy bangles |
| Sangeet | Choker | Secure drops | Bracelet |
| Wedding | Heavy set | Jhumkas or chandbalis | Tikka, bangles, rings |
| Reception | AD statement | AD drops | Cocktail ring |

## Save money without looking repetitive

- Reuse your bridal earrings at the reception with a different necklace.
- Pick bangles in a neutral tone that works across three functions.
- Choose one hero piece per function rather than buying everything new.

## Trial before the day

Wear your full wedding look for at least an hour at home. Check the weight of the earrings, whether the tikka stays in place and whether necklaces catch on your dupatta. It is much easier to fix these at home than on the day.
