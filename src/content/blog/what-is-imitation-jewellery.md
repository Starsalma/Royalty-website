If you have ever bought a pair of jhumkas for a wedding without spending on gold, you have bought imitation jewellery. The name sounds a bit dismissive, but it covers some of the most beautiful and practical jewellery people in India wear today.

## So what is imitation jewellery?

Imitation jewellery is jewellery designed to look like precious jewellery without being made of solid gold, silver or real gemstones. Instead it usually has a base metal such as brass, copper or an alloy, a plated or coloured finish on top, and glass, cubic zirconia, beads or synthetic pearls in place of precious stones.

You will also hear it called **artificial jewellery** or **fashion jewellery**. In practice people use all three names for the same thing.

## Why so many people choose it

- **You can match every outfit.** Weddings now have four or five functions. Having a different set for each one is realistic with imitation jewellery.
- **Less worry when you travel.** You can wear a heavy bridal look to a function without worrying about losing something irreplaceable.
- **Trends move fast.** Styles change every season, and imitation pieces let you try them without a big spend.
- **The designs are genuinely good.** Many imitation pieces copy traditional techniques like kundan and temple work closely.

## The main types you will see

### Kundan

Flat, glass stones set close together in a gold-tone frame, often with pearl drops. Rich and traditional, ideal for weddings. See our [kundan jewellery collection](/collections/kundan-jewellery/).

### American diamond (AD or CZ)

Faceted cubic zirconia stones that sparkle like diamonds. Great for receptions, parties and western outfits. Browse [American diamond jewellery](/collections/american-diamond-jewellery/).

### Temple

Motifs from South Indian temple art, such as deities, peacocks and lotus flowers, usually in an antique gold finish. Made for silk sarees. See [temple jewellery](/collections/temple-jewellery/).

### Oxidised

A darkened silver-tone finish that shows off carved detail. Relaxed and boho, perfect with kurtas and for Navratri. See [oxidised jewellery](/collections/oxidised-jewellery/).

### Antique finish

A matte gold tone that looks like jewellery handed down through the family. See [antique gold jewellery](/collections/antique-jewellery/).

## How to tell a good piece from a poor one

You cannot judge everything from a photo, but a few checks help a lot:

1. **Look at the finish up close.** The colour should be even, without patches or bubbles in the plating.
2. **Check how stones are set.** Stones should sit flat and secure, not glued at an angle.
3. **Check the back.** Neat finishing on the back of a piece usually means care went into the front too.
4. **Ask for a video.** A short video in daylight shows the true colour and sparkle far better than an edited photo.
